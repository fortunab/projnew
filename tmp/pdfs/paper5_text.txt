1. Introduction009
Medical images vary across institutions because of dif-010
ferences in acquisition devices, imaging protocols, patient011
populations, and sample preparation [1]. These variations012
create a persistent challenge for visual recognition: a model013
that performs well in its training environment may fail at an014
unseen medical center. Zech et al. document variable cross-015
hospital performance and hospital-associated confounding016
in pneumonia detection from chest radiographs [13]. The017
challenge extends beyond achieving high average accuracy.018
Reliable recognition requires representations that preserve019
diagnostically relevant visual structure while remaining ro-020
bust to changes in how images are acquired. Multi-center021
datasets such as PolypGen provide an opportunity to inves-022
tigate this problem under explicit institutional distribution023
shifts [1].024
Pretrained visual encoders offer reusable representations025
for downstream recognition, including frozen-feature eval-026
uation [9]. Their benefit in medical imaging is task-027
dependent: Raghu et al. found limited performance gains028
from ImageNet transfer in the two medical tasks they stud-029
ied [10]. However, pretraining alone does not ensure that030
downstream decision rules generalize across centers. A031
classifier learned from pooled source data may exploit ac-032
quisition characteristics that correlate with class labels in033
the training set [13]. Theoretical results in domain adap-034
tation also show that distribution alignment alone need not035
ensure low target error [14]. Whether alignment suppresses036
useful variation in our medical setting remains an empirical037
question. This raises a central question: how can a recogni-038
tion model retain class-discriminative structure without re-039
lying on center-specific visual cues?040
Prototype-based learning provides a natural framework041
for studying this question. By representing classes through042
reference vectors in an embedding space, prototype meth-043
ods make the relationship between representation geometry044
and prediction explicit [11]. Strong cosine-classifier base-045
lines further motivate controlled evaluation of whether a046
proposed prototype mechanism contributes beyond its un-047
derlying representation [3]. Prior work represents cate-048
gories through multiple prototypical image parts, as in Pro-049
toPNet [2]. Here, we study global embedding prototypes050
and hypothesize that a single prototype may compress dis-051
tinct class appearances. The pooled mean also gives greater052
influence to centers contributing more samples, as formal-053
ized in Eq. 2. Introducing multiple prototypes increases rep-054
resentational flexibility, but does not automatically distin-055
guish meaningful within-class variation from acquisition-056
specific structure. Consequently, neither pooled class cen-057
troids nor unconstrained collections of prototypes necessar-058
ily provide a decision rule that transfers to an unseen center.059
We investigatedomain-robust prototype learning for060
medical image recognition. Our guiding hypothesis is that061
prototypes should capture variation that supports class dis-062
crimination while limiting dependence on the composition063
of the source centers. We study this hypothesis using064
pretrained image representations and controlled compar-065
isons between pooled centroids, learned cosine classifiers,066
and multiple-prototype formulations. Center information067
is available during training to characterize source-domain068
variation, while inference must operate without access to069
the identity or labeled examples of the target center. This070
setting isolates generalization to unseen institutions from071
adaptation using target-domain data.072
A rigorous evaluation protocol is essential to assess073
whether improvements reflect transferable visual struc-074
ture. DomainBed emphasizes matched experimental condi-075
tions and source-only selection [6]; nested evaluation sep-076
arates hyperparameter selection from performance estima-077
tion [12]. We therefore formulate the study around held-078

2. Background and Related Work118
2.1. Cross-Center Domain Generalization119
Following the domain-generalization setting of Do-120
mainBed [6], we model each imaging center as a domain121
with a joint distributionPs(X, Y)over imagesXand labels122
Y. Given labeled datasets fromSsource centers, the objec-123
tive is to learn a predictor that transfers to an unseen target124
distributionP t(X, Y)sharing the same label space. Cross-125
hospital evaluation demonstrates differences in image ap-126
pearance and class prevalence [13], while class-conditional127
shift matters in analyses of transfer [14]. We therefore treat128
center identity as a grouping variable rather than a complete129
description of acquisition style. In this paper, target-center130
images are unavailable during training and model selection.131
This is a domain-generalization setting [6], whereas unsu-132
pervised domain adaptation permits access to unlabeled tar-133
get images, as in the medical segmentation setting of En and134
Guo [5].135
Empirical risk minimization (ERM) on pooled source136
data is an essential baseline. Gulrajani and Lopez-Paz [6]137
demonstrate that careful implementation and model selec-138
tion can make ERM competitive with dedicated domain-139
generalization algorithms on the benchmarks they examine.140
Their findings motivate matched architectures, tuning bud-141
gets, and source-only validation rather than assuming that142
an additional alignment objective is beneficial. Separately,143
Zhao et al. [14] show, in domain adaptation, that distribution144
invariance and low source error do not suffice for successful145
transfer under certain shifts. These results caution against146
indiscriminate alignment; they do not prove that alignment147
necessarily harms the medical tasks considered here.148

3. Methodology230
3.1. Setting and Design Rationale231
We formulate the proposed method ascenter-balanced232
multi-prototype classification(CBMP). The training set233
contains tuples(x i, yi, si, gi), wheres i denotes the source234
center andg i identifies a patient or video group. The target235
center is excluded from feature fitting, prototype construc-236
tion, and hyperparameter selection. All centers share a fixed237
label vocabulary; every evaluated class must occur in the238
source training set. For PolypGen classification, labels indi-239
cate polyp presence, subject to an audited image-label man-240
ifest rather than assumptions based solely on folder names.241
The core experiment freezes a pretrained encoderf θ and242
learns only class prototypes. This choice separates proto-243
type construction from representation adaptation. CBMP244
changes the influence of centers in class-conditional clus-245
tering and represents each class with several reference di-246
rections. It imposes no explicit domain-invariance loss and247
makes no assumption that center identity fully explains vi-248
sual variation. Whether its weighting improves transfer is249
tested against identically encoded baselines.250
Figure 1 summarizes the system and its experimental251
data boundaries. The same outer center split and encoder252
features are used for CBMP and its controls. Inner source-253
center validation selects the configuration before the outer254
source data are used to construct the final prototype bank.255
Target images enter only the fixed inference path.256
